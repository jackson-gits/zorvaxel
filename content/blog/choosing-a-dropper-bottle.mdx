Choosing a dropper bottle is not just a matter of picking a size and colour. The bottle, pipette, bulb, cap and formula need to work together. Testing the full package before ordering can help catch dispensing problems, leaks or a closure that does not suit everyday use.

## Start with the serum’s texture

Viscosity describes how thick or runny a liquid is. A watery serum may travel through a pipette quickly, while a thicker or oil-rich formula may draw slowly or remain in the tube. Ask which pipette options suit your actual formula, then test a filled sample instead of relying on appearance alone.

Check whether the dropper draws and releases the product consistently. Try it upright and at different angles. If the formula contains suspended ingredients or needs shaking, make sure the dropper handles it and that any directions are clear. Ask the manufacturer to review compatibility for the formula and all materials that touch it.

## Check material and light requirements

Do not assume every glass bottle is suitable for every serum. Confirm the bottle material, coating, decoration and contacting parts with the manufacturer. Ask what compatibility checks are available for your formula and how long a sample can be observed before approval.

Amber or tinted glass can be considered when light exposure matters, but colour alone is not proof of protection. Ask for relevant packaging information and consider how the product will be stored and shipped. Select a pack based on the formula’s needs rather than appearance alone.

## Match the fill to the bottle

Bottle capacity and the amount of product filled are not the same. The dropper assembly takes up space, and the pipette should reach enough of the product as the bottle is used. Ask for the recommended fill volume, the fill line and the expected headspace. Check how much serum remains when the dropper can no longer draw it up.

Confirm the stated net contents and bottle dimensions before approving artwork. A bottle that looks large may not hold the fill you expect once the closure is installed. Request a sample of the exact bottle and closure combination you plan to use.

## Test the complete closure

The pipette must fit the neck and work with its bulb and cap as one assembly. Confirm the tip length, squeeze feel and dispensing amount. Do not assume one dropper fits every bottle, even if the neck size appears similar.

Fill samples with your serum, close them as instructed, and store them upright and on their side. Check for leaks, residue around the neck, loose caps and changes in dispensing. Ask the manufacturer what closure or leak checks are appropriate for the pack before signing off on the production sample.

## Plan labels and the outer carton

Small bottles leave limited space for readable product information. Check the label area, curvature and material, then ask for a decorated sample before approving artwork. If you use direct printing, confirm the surface and finish. For a carton, test the filled bottle and dropper together so the package fits securely and the information matches across both pieces.

## Use a short sampling checklist

Before ordering, test the exact bottle, pipette, bulb, cap and decoration with your own formula. Record dispensing, fill volume, remaining product, closure fit, leakage, compatibility observations and label readability. Confirm that production materials will match the sample. Standard stock bottles with labels may be a simpler starting point than custom shapes, but check the manufacturer’s available options and minimums.

The best dropper is the one that suits the serum, works consistently, closes securely and carries readable information. Test the full assembly before approving a run, and keep the final package specification with your production records.
